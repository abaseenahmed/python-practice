HOUSE PRICE DATASET — EDA AND CLEANING REPORT
====================================================

Dataset scope
- Raw observations and fields: 4,600 rows x 18 columns
- Cleaned observations and fields: 4,551 rows x 18 columns
- Sale date range: 2014-05-02 to 2014-07-10
- Exact duplicate rows found: 0
- Missing cells in raw input: 0

Target analysis
- Non-positive/missing sale-price rows removed: 49
- Median positive sale price: $465,000
- Mean positive sale price: $557,906
- Positive-price skewness: 25.02
- Positive-price range: $7,800 to $26,590,000
- Price observations beyond the 1.5xIQR fences: 240 (5.22%)

Data quality findings and treatment
- Rows removed for exact duplication: 0.
- Rows excluded for a non-positive/missing target: 49.
- Invalid sale dates removed: 0.
- Invalid renovation years (before construction or after sale): 193; treated as no reliable renovation evidence.
- Zero bedroom values set to missing: 2.
- Zero bathroom values set to missing: 2.
- `street` was excluded because it is a near-unique address identifier; `country` was excluded because it is constant.
- Sale date was decomposed into sale year/month; construction and renovation years were represented as house age, a renovation indicator, and years since renovation.
- Missing feature values are retained. Fit imputers on training folds only to avoid validation/test leakage.

Potential outliers
- IQR fences are a screening device, not proof of data error. No positive-price observations were removed solely for being extreme.
- Inspect the extreme-price and large-lot records against source/business context before deciding on a transformation, robust loss, or exclusion rule.

Geographic coverage
- Most frequent cities: Seattle (1,573), Renton (293), Bellevue (286), Redmond (235), Issaquah (187)

Training recommendations
- Define `price` as the target and use a train/validation/test split before fitting imputation, scaling, encoding, or feature selection.
- Keep `city` and `statezip` as categorical features and encode them inside a training pipeline; check category coverage in held-out data.
- Evaluate target skew and residuals; compare a raw-price model with a log-target approach using metrics reported in dollars.
- Consider a geographic-aware split or grouped validation because homes in nearby areas can share pricing patterns.
